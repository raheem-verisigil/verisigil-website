EU AI Act — Article 25 · Value-Chain Governance

Who is responsible for your AI
across the value chain?

Modern enterprises rarely have one AI provider. They have models, applications, vendors, integrators, internal teams and deployers operating across the same AI lifecycle. Article 25 of the EU AI Act addresses where responsibility begins, shifts, and overlaps.

Important distinction: VeriSigil AI does not provide legal advice or declare an organisation legally compliant with Article 25. It provides governance infrastructure to help organisations map, govern and evidence AI responsibilities across the value chain. Legal determination of obligations requires qualified legal advice.
When Downstream Actors Acquire Provider Obligations — Article 25

Article 25 recognises that responsibility is not fixed at the point of creation. Downstream actors can become subject to provider obligations under specified circumstances.

Own Name / Trademark
A distributor or deployer that puts its own name or trademark on a high-risk AI system takes on provider obligations for that system.
Substantial Modification
An actor that makes a substantial modification to a high-risk AI system before placing it on the market becomes responsible as a provider.
Third-Country Import
An importer placing a third-country AI system on the EU market acquires provider obligations where the original provider cannot be held to account.
Places on Market
An actor placing a high-risk AI system on the market under its own name, regardless of the original provider, takes on provider obligations.
